# Purchasing-Oversight

Tools: Python, SQL, Tableau, ChatGPT, and Excel


This project was inspired by my current role and my desire to simplify and streamline processes. Although smaller in scale than the data I handle daily, I designed it to help even the smallest companies I work with, while keeping it scalable for larger branches.

My team enters invoices for multiple vendors across 8 HVAC and Plumbing companies nationwide. Early on, I noticed patterns in technician purchasing behaviors. More specifically, I noticed a tendency to favor a particular vendor, even when other vendors offered the same parts at lower prices. As I dug deeper, it became clear there was a gap in monitoring purchases, which could lead to risks like unauthorized side work, inaccurate cost tracking by job, and outdated pricing for our salesmen.

Since this type of analysis isn’t part of my current role, I built a side project to simulate how I’d approach this issue-leveraging the skills from my Data Analytics Certification and self-study, while keeping my company's existing entry process intact. Realizing open-source data wouldn’t reflect my real-world experience, I generated realistic (but clearly fake) invoices using Python and ChatGPT, naming the vendors after my pets. I also created fictional work orders in Excel, featuring TV characters as customers, to simulate data from our scheduling software.

I used SQL and Excel to analyze and manipulate the purchasing data and built visualizations in Excel and Tableau. Finally, I assembled a slide deck with some of the created visualizations to simulate presenting findings to stakeholders. In the slide deck, I explain what I discovered, recommended steps to enact, and outlined future opportunities to leverage this underutilized data.

Key Insights and Benefits:

Leveraging the costs associated to each job, we can closely monitor technician purchases, use the data to negotiate better vendor pricing, perform cost of goods analysis with pin-point accuracy, and help make informed stocking decisions.

Closely monitoring price changes as they happen, we can update our pricing to ensure profitable and competitive estimates can be provided to our customers.

Historical purchasing data can drive smarter marketing and operations. It helps identify when customers buy specific services, allowing teams to create timely promotions that boost sales without hurting margins. (For example, it isn't beneficial to offer a water heater coupon when parts are at peak cost.)

Managers can use the same data, when formatted in a presentable way, to stock high-cost parts when prices are low and optimize staffing during slower seasons.

Accounts Payable benefits from faster invoice entry, simplified statement reconciliation, and added value from "the people who spend money."
