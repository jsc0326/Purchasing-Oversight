# Purchasing-Oversight

Tools: Python, SQL, Tableau, ChatGPT, and Excel

The inspiration for this project comes from experience in my current role and my desire to make things easier and more efficient. This is a small scale project compared to the data I encounter on a daily basis, but I approached this as a way to help the smallest of the companies I do data entry for, while keeping it scalable for the larger branches. My team enters invoices for multiple vendors for 8 HVAC and Plumbing companies across the United States. When I first started in this role, I immediately began to notice patterns within technician purchasing behaviors, more specifically, favoring certain vendors over others. Vendor A had significantly more purchases when compared to some of the other vendors. As my familiarity with the various part names grew, I began to notice that some parts were significantly cheaper at other vendors, yet the technicians were still purchasing from Vendor A. I couldn't help but wonder why. I started asking questions that ultimately indicated that I had found a gap in monitoring purchases, which posed risks such as unauthorized side work, inaccuarate cost tracking, and outdated pricing. While exploring purchasing data, primarily with pen and paper, I decided I wanted to be more efficient at this task.

Since this type of work was not a part of my current position, I decided to go to work on a project that would simulate, on a small scale, how I would approach addressing this problem, while keeping the current entry process the same. I wanted to challenge myself to use the skills I had aquired from my Data Analytics Certification and countless hours of self-educating, without creating additional work or steps for my theoretical teammates. After looking for data in all the usual places like Kaggle, I quickly realized I was not going to find data similar to what I experience on a daily basis, especially in an open-source format. I realized the best way for me to show that I had identified a problem I intened to fix, I had to create and structure the data myself. I genarated realistic but clearly fake invoices, complete with vendor names inspired by my pets, using Python and some help from chatGPT. These invoices contain the same information I would receive on a real-life invoice, except for the comically high prices. After creating these ai generated invoices to simulate a month's worth of purchases at a very small branch for my company, I used Excel create fictional work orders, featuring TV characters as customers, to simulate the data I would be able to pull from our scheduling software. Next, I used SQL and Excel to further manipulate and analyze the purchasing data. Then, I used Excel and Tableau to create visualizations. Lastly, I put together a small slide deck, to simulate the presentation I would give to stakeholders explaning what I found, what we should do about it, and what I want to do next to further leverage the under-utilized data to help make decisions.

This proof of concept demonstrates analyzing purchasing data can provide valuable insights to multiple departments.

  Managers can track technician purchases, negotiate better pricing from their venders, analyze cost of goods sold, and make educated decisions when it comes to stocked items.

  Field Technicians can offer competitive, profitable estimates while helping other departments better understand their purchasing behaviors.

  Accounts Payable benefits from saving time during invoice entry, simplified statement reconciliation, and added value from a team who is jokingly referred to as, "The people who spend my money."
